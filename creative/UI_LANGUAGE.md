# Captionflow UI Language for Motion

The motion pieces should borrow the **logic** of Captionflow's interface, not repeatedly recreate the same screenshot.

## Product structure to preserve

Captionflow is visually built around:
- a dark workspace
- a large video/content stage
- compact side controls
- subtitle/style/export panels
- timeline language
- active violet/blue states
- small precise labels and metadata
- white primary actions when maximum contrast is needed

## Component behavior

### Panels
Use deep dark translucent surfaces with very subtle borders. Panels can enter through scale, mask, slide or perspective, but their resting state should remain clean.

### Buttons
Primary actions can be white on black for maximum clarity. Secondary actions should feel integrated into the dark UI. Accent purple is for selection, emphasis and active state rather than every CTA.

### Tabs and segmented controls
Quiet inactive states, clear active state. A tiny underline, violet-tinted surface or crisp contrast shift is preferable to heavy pills everywhere.

### Timeline
Timeline language is a useful visual motif:
- horizontal clip/caption blocks
- thin glowing playhead
- compact timecode
- selected segment with restrained violet fill/border

Use this when it strengthens the story. Do not force a timeline into every video.

### Captions
Captionflow's signature caption behavior may be represented by:
- clean white text
- active word emphasis
- violet/blue or approved Signature gradient
- thin crisp outline when needed for footage legibility
- no blurry halo around the text itself

### Cards
Cards should exist because they represent a real concept: asset, clip, preset, export, generated media, transcript item, setting, etc. Avoid decorative cards with meaningless labels.

### Icons
Use simple line icons in the visual spirit of Lucide. Small iconography should remain secondary to text and product states.

## Spatial language

The real product often combines:
- one dominant surface
- one supporting control region
- one narrow metadata/timeline zone

Motion videos may deconstruct these into layers. Preserve the feeling of a coherent product system even when pieces float in 3D space.

## Authenticity rule

If a video invents UI to communicate a future or abstract feature, it must still look as though the same Captionflow design team made it.

Use the same:
- palette
- type hierarchy
- border opacity
- surface darkness
- rounding logic
- spacing discipline
- active-state language

Never create generic dashboard widgets, analytics charts or fake controls simply to make a frame look 'tech'.

## Mobile legibility

For 9:16 output:
- avoid reproducing a full desktop app at unreadable scale
- crop intelligently into the useful part of the interface
- enlarge controls when they carry the story
- allow UI to become cinematic rather than literal
- keep essential labels readable without zooming the phone screen

The viewer should understand the product interaction without needing to inspect tiny interface details.
